## Assessment
1. **usable** – Claim, unverified: 'Everyone' and 'almost no one' are absolutes.
2. **not usable** – The stated aim is that people "feel dumb saying no": shaming and pressure to buy. Rule 7: a mechanism is not allowed when the aim is to move a person against their own interest; order of authority 1 (no humiliation, no pressure into a purchase). The same sentence is rated not usable in station 37 statement 1. 'Will make' is also an unproven promise of effect (rule 5).
3. **usable** – Name of the method.
4. **usable** – Connective.
5. **conditional** – The formula (product or feature + "so you can" + outcome) is usable. Required change: the outcome named must be what the product really delivers, for the typical customer, and the product itself and its terms stay stated (rules 1, 3, 5).
6. **conditional** – The wording is usable. Required change: "feel safe" must not promise protection beyond what the product does (rules 1, 5). The picture used for it (an intruder in a dark house) works through fear: fear no stronger than the real size of the problem (rule 7), and a person's silhouette is not usable in own content (rule 9).
7. **conditional** – Required change: "no distractions" is an absolute; noise-cancelling reduces noise, not every distraction. State what is true, for example "fewer distractions" (rules 1, 5). The man in the photo is not usable in own content (rule 9).
8. **conditional** – Claim, unverified. Required change: "stop selling the product" must not mean hiding what is sold, the price or the terms (rule 3, gharar); the outcome must be a real need of the customer, not one invented for them (rule 7).
9. **usable** – Call to action to comment; platform use is allowed (rule 12). No checkpoint touched.

Counts: usable 4 (1, 3, 4, 9), conditional 4 (5, 6, 7, 8), not usable 1 (2).
