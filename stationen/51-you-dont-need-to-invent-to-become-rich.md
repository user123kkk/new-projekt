## Assessment
1. **conditional** – The core (inventing something is not required) is usable. Required change: in own content do not promise that anything leads to riches (rules 1, 5). Whether becoming rich is adopted as a goal is not covered by a GRUNDSATZ rule; it is for the owner. Claim, unverified.
2. **usable** – Claim, unverified: no data on why people do not start a business. Usable as a description of a belief.
3. **usable** – Connective.
4. **usable** – Claim, unverified. Core of the video; compare [[04-there-is-no-winning-product]] (K6 in IDEEN.md: the product does not need to be unique).
5. **conditional** – Strategy usable. Required change: "5% better" is an invented measure; any "better" claim about our product needs a defined, checkable improvement and proof (rule 5), for example a stated delivery time or a stated price. Copying the protected work of another company (name, design, text) is a separate legal question; no GRUNDSATZ rule covers it.
6. **usable** – Hypothetical.
7. **usable** – Method: read real customer complaints instead of guessing; matches [[14-sell-water-to-the-thirsty]] statement 2 and the demand search in NACHFRAGE.md. No checkpoint touched.
8. **usable** – Examples of complaints ("Maybe"); hypothetical.
9. **conditional** – Required change: "better than the competition" is a comparative claim and needs proof (rule 5); no fake or misleading comparison (rule 4, najsh; see 26 statement 8); other known weaknesses of the offer are not hidden (rule 1). Solving one problem is a reason to choose, not a guarantee.
10. **conditional** – Existing spending shows demand for the existing product, not for our version. Required change: it is not proof of demand for our offer; "you don't have to guess" is too absolute (rule 1: no claim without basis). The product must be permissible (rule 15; order of authority 1) and not a religious obligation sold as a gap (rule 11).
11. **usable** – Honest limit on the previous claims; supports rule 1 (no promise of success).

14. **usable** – Claim, unverified.
