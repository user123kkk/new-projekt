## Assessment
1. **conditional** – 'Psychology says' names no source; claim, unverified. Required change: the negative is a real fact (price, limit, risk) that is stated completely, not a rhetorical device to cover something else (rule 3, gharar; rule 7).
2. **conditional** – The ordering is the example. 'The best in the world' is a superlative that needs proof (rule 5). Required change: replace it with a claim that can be proven.
3. **conditional** – Same as statement 2: ordering usable; 'the best in the world' only with proof (rule 5). 'Really expensive' states the price honestly, which is usable (rule 3).
4. **usable** – Announces the next example; no checkpoint touched.
5. **conditional** – Core is a money-back guarantee (see [[guarantee]]). Required change: the guarantee must be real, honoured, with plain conditions: period, what counts as 'the results you want', how to claim and how fast the refund comes (rule 1, rule 3, gharar). 'Results you want' is subjective; define it.
6. **conditional** – Same promise in a different order; same condition as statement 5.
7. **conditional** – Usable as an order for true information. Required change: the negative (price, conditions, limits) is stated fully and clearly and is not drowned by a positive ending; the positive ending is true (rule 3, rule 7, rule 1).
8. **usable** – Claim, unverified (no study named): the last thing said is remembered most. Note: contradicts [[30-content-fails-in-the-first-seconds]] ('the beginning matters even more than the end').
