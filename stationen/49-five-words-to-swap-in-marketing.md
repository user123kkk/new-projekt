## Assessment
1. **usable** – Claim, unverified; hyperbole ("kill"). The image of a burning Blockbuster store implies that wording caused its failure; this is not stated in the audio and is not adopted.
2. **usable** – Advice about a button or call-to-action word. No checkpoint touched.
3. **conditional** – Required change: the label must say what really happens. If "start now" leads to account creation, a payment or a trial that turns into a subscription, that must be clear before the click (rule 3, gharar; rule 6, no dark pattern). "Sign up" is not wrong when an account is created.
4. **usable** – Consistent with rule 5: "best" only with proof.
5. **conditional** – Required change: "top rated" is a claim of ranking by ratings. Allowed only with real ratings, and the source and number of reviews disclosed (rules 1, 5). A swap of "best" for "top rated" does not remove the need for proof.
6. **usable** – Consistent with rules 1 and 5: "only here" must not be said if it is not true.
7. **conditional** – Required change: "exclusive" says the same as "only here" in other words. It is allowed only if the offer is really not available elsewhere. Using it to say the same untrue claim in a softer word is deception (rules 1, 5).
8. **usable** – Advice about a word. A real price reduction can still be named truthfully.
9. **conditional** – Required change: "special offer" must refer to a real reduction or real extra. No invented "was" price, no fake deadline (rules 1, 4, najsh). Using it as a euphemism for "sale" where nothing is reduced is not allowed.
10. **usable** – Consistent with rule 5: "must have" is an exaggeration without proof.
11. **conditional** – Required change: "essential" claims necessity. Only if the product is truly necessary for the customer's stated goal (rule 5). Otherwise the exaggeration of "must have" is only reworded.

Counts: usable 6 (1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10), conditional 5 (3, 5, 7, 9, 11), not usable 0.
